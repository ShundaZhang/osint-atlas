01 / FIELD NOTES

Open sources become intelligence through analysis

Start with a question that a reader can evaluate.

OSINT is the collection and analysis of openly obtainable information to answer a defined question. A pile of search results is information; a supported conclusion explains what the sources establish, what they cannot establish, and why it matters to the question. Open-source intelligence is unrelated to whether a software repository is open source.

A useful starting question is “Does this image show the public bridge named in its caption?” Separate that from “Who took it?” and “What happened there?” The same picture may support the first question while leaving the others unresolved.

02 / FIELD NOTES

A small collection plan prevents aimless browsing

Define the decision, scope, information gaps and stopping condition.

FieldExample
QuestionWhich of three public landmarks matches the scene?
Inputs & scopeOriginal image, claimed city, public map imagery; no personal identity investigation
Needed evidenceRoad–river topology, building shape, independently captured street imagery
Stop / reviseStop at a documented match or state an unresolved region; widen the scope only after testing the claim

Keep an evidence ledger while you search. Record unsuccessful queries and rejected candidates too: they make it possible to resume a case and show that the favorite answer was actually tested.
