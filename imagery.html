01 / FIELD NOTES

Choose the sensor for the size of the question

Zooming enlarges pixels; it does not add captured detail.

Sentinel-2 optical bands have nominal 10, 20 or 60 metre spatial sampling. They are useful for land cover and sufficiently large changes, not reading small signs or identifying people. NASA Worldview layers vary widely in resolution and cadence; broad smoke or flood patterns are a better fit than a single doorway.

RGB rendering is a chosen interpretation of bands. False-color and radar products can reveal different properties, but require different reasoning. Do not apply the same color assumptions to optical and radar imagery, or confuse pixel spacing with guaranteed object recognition.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Capture date, mosaic date and access date differ

Record which date the product actually exposes.

Google Earth imagery can be assembled from images acquired on different dates; 3D textures also need careful interpretation. A historical timeline label is not a guarantee of one exact capture instant for every pixel. Read the displayed imagery metadata and retain the area around the evidence.

In Copernicus Browser or EarthExplorer, save the scene/product identifier, acquisition time, band selection and cloud information. A platform upload time or the date printed in a news caption is a separate claim. Clouds can hide a feature, so “not visible” is not always “absent”.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Compare like with like before claiming a change

Season, clouds, sensor and viewing angle can explain differences.

Use a before/after pair with comparable resolution, band combination, location and season where possible. Inspect shadows, water levels and vegetation. Align recognizable stable structures and document any reprojection or resampling. Keep original products beside your annotated comparison.

For a large new roof, report when it was first observed and the interval between the last clear absence and first clear presence. That bounds the visible change; it does not automatically give the construction start date or cause.
