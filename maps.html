01 / FIELD NOTES

Use map products for different kinds of evidence

A labeled map, satellite image and street panorama are not interchangeable.

ViewBest useCheck
Labeled road / POI mapNames, route structure and administrative contextPin placement and naming source
Pure overhead imageryFootprints, water, road geometry and surrounding contextDate, north orientation, stitching and labels turned off
Street panoramaGround signs, facade, occlusion and camera-side matchCapture date, panorama origin, camera marker and orientation
3D viewTerrain and broad perspective comparisonModel artifacts and mixed-date textures

02 / FIELD NOTES

Search OpenStreetMap by relationships

Query a small region, then inspect the surrounding geometry.

Bellingcat OSM Search can find combinations of mapped features. Overpass Turbo allows more explicit queries. Nominatim resolves names and addresses; it is not an image-geolocation engine. A missing mapped feature may simply be unmapped.

[out:json][timeout:25];
(
  way["bridge"]({{bbox}});
  way["railway"="rail"]({{bbox}});
);
out geom;

Paste this teaching query into Overpass Turbo after zooming to a small area. It returns bridge-tagged ways and rail ways; it does not itself prove they are close together, that every bridge is mapped, or that any site matches an image. Compare the geometry and refine the query.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Use regional maps without mixing their coordinates

Provider coverage and local names can be better than a single global map.

For mainland China, compare AMap or Baidu naming with available street imagery and overhead geometry. Keep a labeled view for names and a separate pure-imagery view for geometry. Road/POI overlays may not align with the underlying imagery in another product. Verify with recognizable intersections rather than shifting everything by an assumed offset.

If one service is blocked, use a different original provider for the required evidence type. Mapillary and KartaView can supply community ground images where covered, but coverage and account requirements vary. Absence on a second site is not evidence that the place does not exist.
