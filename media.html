01 / FIELD NOTES

Reverse image search answers reuse questions

Search both the whole image and distinctive crops.

Lens and TinEye can surface similar or indexed copies. Compare uncropped edges, resolution, visible signs and accompanying text. A match with an older caption is a lead: read the older page and verify whether its scene and date are actually supported.

A missing match does not prove authenticity. Cropping, mirroring, new uploads and limited indexes can hide copies. If upload to an external service is inappropriate for the material, use local inspection and textual clue searches instead.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Inspect a video as a sequence, not a single thumbnail

Track continuity, camera motion and keyframe timestamps.

Select frames where signs, roads and distant structures become clear. Keep each frame’s timestamp and a link to the source video. Motion can reveal whether a landmark is behind another object or whether two cuts represent different locations. Audio may provide leads, but captions and voiceovers can be added later.

InVID helps organize verification tasks; Hugin can combine overlapping frames for scene inspection. A panorama is a derived image with warping and possibly moving objects. Keep raw frames beside the panorama so the transformation remains visible.

Read the source: InVID verification plugin ↗ · Hugin ↗

03 / FIELD NOTES

Metadata is a clue with a provenance problem

Inspect the original bytes before making copies or edits.

ExifTool can read embedded camera, GPS and time fields. Preserve its output alongside the original file. EXIF time may lack a time zone, GPS can be absent or edited, and a filesystem modification time may describe download rather than capture. Messaging platforms often strip metadata.

Compression artifacts, inconsistent lighting and generative-image detectors can prompt further checks, but none alone establishes fabrication. Verify the source chain and physical scene; keep “not verified” distinct from “fake”.

exiftool -G1 -a -s original.jpg
