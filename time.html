01 / FIELD NOTES

Build time bounds before estimating an exact moment

Different timestamps describe different events.

TimestampMeaningCaution
Capture / eventWhen the depicted scene was recorded or occurredMetadata and captions need verification
Upload / publicationWhen a platform or publisher made a copy availableMay be much later than the scene
Archive captureWhen an archive recorded an accessible responseNot necessarily the first publication
Research retrievalWhen you obtained the evidenceOnly describes your acquisition

A completed building, road closure or festival banner can supply a time bound if its own dated record is reliable. State “after X, before Y” when that is what the evidence supports; do not turn a broad interval into a single date.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Shadows test compatibility with a solar position

Establish true north and the shadow’s physical plane first.

For a vertical object on level ground, the shadow points away from the solar azimuth. If the sun’s azimuth is A, the shadow bearing is approximately (A + 180°) mod 360°. In the same ideal model, shadow length is h / tan(α), where h is object height and α solar elevation. These are not direct formulas for arbitrary photo pixels.

SunCalc lets you compare a location, date and time. Check time zone, daylight saving and azimuth convention. Slope, nonvertical objects, occlusion and perspective change what the image shows. Record a range of plausible dates/times rather than a falsely exact answer.

03 / FIELD NOTES

A matching shadow is rarely a unique timestamp

Several dates and locations may yield similar geometry.

Solar paths repeat seasonally, and a single bearing leaves many unknowns. Combine shadow evidence with a known location, independent time bounds and several trustworthy observations. Weather and vegetation are supporting context with geographic and seasonal overlap.

NOAA’s legacy solar calculator is no longer actively maintained; its documentation is useful for calculation assumptions. Cross-check current ephemeris tools instead of treating one calculator output as certified evidence. If no independent bounds exist, leave the time question unresolved.
