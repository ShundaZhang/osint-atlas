01 / FIELD NOTES

Resolve entities before drawing relationships

Names are labels; identifiers and dated records support identity.

For an organization, compare jurisdiction, registration number, official domains and historical names. For public accounts, a similar handle is only a candidate. Store the exact record and date of each relation: “listed as director in filing X” is more precise than “controls company Y”.

Official registries are valuable anchors but may be delayed, self-reported or limited to one jurisdiction. Do not merge entities just because their names or profile pictures match. Separate identity confidence from confidence in a specific claimed relationship.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Infrastructure evidence supports narrow claims

Different records describe different layers and dates.

SourceCan supportCannot establish alone
Registration / RDAPPublic registrar and registration fields returned at lookup timeThe real-world operator behind redacted data
DNS recordA resolution observed at a specified timeOwnership from shared hosting or a CDN IP
CT certificate logA logged certificate contains specified namesCurrent service, compromise or common ownership

Lookups to published data sources are not an active test of the underlying server. Keep observation time, response and query method. Historical names may be retired; wildcard certificates do not enumerate every actual host.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Use a sourced graph to expose gaps

Every edge needs a type, date and evidence ID.

Model nodes as distinct entities and edges as claims such as “publishes”, “registered in” or “appears in filing”. Attach the underlying source and confidence to each edge. A shared address can be a service provider; a visual cluster can be created by your layout algorithm.

Gephi is useful for exploration and presentation, but calculate metrics only after examining coverage and duplicates. Show unresolved identities separately. A reproducible small graph is often more useful than a dramatic chart with thousands of unsupported edges.
